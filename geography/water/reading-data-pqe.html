Reading data

Set the students' version in OneNote.

P · Q · E

Any time you describe a graph, chart or map, do these three things, in this order.

P: Pattern
The general trend. What's the overall story? Use direction words (rises, falls, from… to…).
Q: Quantify
Prove it with numbers. Quote 2–3 real figures, with the units and the place.
E: Exception
The odd one out, or the surprise the pattern hides.

the exception

Inside Australia: the water is in the north; the people are in the south-east

This is the exception. The national average (≈21,000 m³) hides a mismatch: most of the water is in the north, where almost no one lives, while the crowded south-east is far drier. That's why a “water-rich” country still builds desalination plants.

Water = share of national runoff (Northern Australia ≈60%, CSIRO). People = share of population (ABS). Rounded/illustrative.
no crisis

Inside Canada: leans north like Australia, but the populated south is wet too

Same skew, no crisis. Canada's water also leans to the empty north, but even its crowded southern belt still gets tens of thousands of m³ per person. There's plenty of water where Canadians actually live, so the high average doesn't mislead the way Australia's does.

Illustrative shares. ~90% of Canadians live within ~300 km of the US border.
regular

Inside Vietnam: the water and the people are in the same place

Regular. Water and people both cluster in the Red River and Mekong deltas, the bars line up. Here the national average tells roughly the truth, because the water is where the people are.

Illustrative shares.
water from abroad

Inside Egypt: almost all the water is the Nile, and it starts in another country

Water from abroad. People follow the Nile, so inside Egypt the water and the people match. The catch is upstream: about 97% of that water is rain that fell in Ethiopia. “Renewable” isn't the same as “secure.”

Egypt ≈97% of water from the Nile (Egyptian Govt / Wikipedia). Rounded.
uniformly scarce

Inside Jordan: scarce almost everywhere

Uniformly scarce. There's no wet region to fall back on: Jordan is among the driest countries on Earth, all over. Its low average isn't hiding anything; the whole place is dry.

≈120 m³ per person (World Bank). Absolute-scarcity line = 500 m³/person/yr.

Explain (SHEEPTSHEEPT: factors to explain why: Social · Historical · Environmental · Economic · Political · Technological.): The pattern is mostly environmental: 's cool, wet climate and huge rivers give enormous runoff, while sit in hot deserts where little rain falls. There is a political-geography twist too: much of 's water is the Nile flowing in from other countries, so its “renewable” water is not fully its own.

It hides distribution within a country: 's water is in the empty north, not the crowded south, which is exactly why it desalinates, and it can count water that depends on a neighbour's rainfall. So “renewable” does not always mean available, or reliable.
