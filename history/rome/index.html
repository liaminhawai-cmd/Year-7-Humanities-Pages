Pliny on Vesuvius

A Roman source, analysed at every level. Extension — this CAT does not assess source analysis.

This wall rehearses CAT 2, and it is not a source-analysis wall. Every other History wall here marks up an analytical paragraph about a source. Ancient Rome does not assess that. Its CAT is a tourist guidebook, marked on chronology, cause and effect, metacognition and presentation — so the worked examples are guidebook writing, and a student comparing their draft with this wall is comparing like with like.

Two of the four rows are not History descriptors. Chronology and cause and effect carry VC 2.0 codes. Metacognition belongs to Critical and Creative Thinking, and Presentation is the school's own reporting row; neither has a curriculum code, and the wall says so rather than inventing one.
