# Source notice: the Cai Lun wall

`cai-lun-papermaking-1637.jpg` is the third of five papermaking woodcuts in the
Tiangong Kaiwu (The Exploitation of the Works of Nature) by Song Yingxing,
printed in 1637 in the Ming dynasty. It shows a worker dipping a screen into a
vat of pulp; its label reads 蕩料入簾.

The file is Wikimedia Commons "Five Steps of Papermaking - Step 3 - Casting
the Paper Pulp - As described by Cai Lun in 105 CE.jpg" (1087x1479, tagged
public domain, credited there to a collection of reproductions of Ming
woodcuts), resized here to 800x1089. The woodcut is nearly 400 years old and
out of copyright everywhere.

The Commons title says the steps are "as described by Cai Lun in 105 CE". That
is wrong: the woodcut shows Ming papermaking, which begins with cutting bamboo,
while the records say Cai Lun used bark, hemp, rags and nets. The wall and the
paper sheet use that gap as the point of the lesson. The title is not repeated
on any page.

The wall is hidden: not in `tools/build_index_pages.py`, noindex, opened by its
address. Students write on paper (Ancient China lesson 2.1, in the private
repository); the teacher shows this ladder on the screen. The students' own
figure, Zhang Qian, is deliberately not on this site.
