# Source notice: the 2.4 wall, The Three Vinegar Tasters

`bump-it-up.html` is the bump-it-up wall for Ancient China lesson 2.4, the three
ways (Confucianism, Taoism, Buddhism). Linked from the China page's Lessons
section (in `tools/build_index_pages.py`). `bump-it-up-standalone.html` is the
one-file copy (`py tools/build_standalone.py history/three-ways/bump-it-up.html`).
The engine is a copy of `history/fu-hao/bump-it-up.html` with its own password.

`content.js` is GENERATED by `tools/gen_three_ways_wall.js`. Edit the generator
and rerun it. It copies the rubric rows, task map and curriculum codes word for
word from `history/fu-hao/content.js`.

## Why this source

In 2.4 each student analyses a source on their own tradition: Source C (the
Admonitions Scroll, Confucianism), Source D (Laozi Riding an Ox, Taoism) and
Source B (the Diamond Sutra, Buddhism). Those sheets are in the private
repository. This wall models the moves on a fourth source that no group has, so
no group is handed a model answer for its own source.

## The picture

`vinegar-tasters.jpg` is Wikimedia Commons "The Three Vinegar Tasters
(cropped).jpg": a 16th-century Japanese hanging scroll by an artist of the Kanō
school, 83.3 x 47.5 cm, Tokyo National Museum. Public domain. The hotspot boxes
in the generator are percentages of this 644 x 1174 file.

## Four tabs and the password

Tab A, The Three Vinegar Tasters, is the teacher model and is open at every level:
no student writes about it. Tabs C, D and B are the students' own sources (the
same pictures and facts as their printed source pages), with worked examples at
every level in `tools/three-ways-sources.js`. Those stay hidden until the teacher
types "harmony" near the end, so students can check their own answer. Every
level's rubric rows show from the start. The examples are still in `content.js`,
so a student who opens the file can read them.

The engine here differs from the Fu Hao copy in one way: a source can carry its
own worked examples, facts and question, and be marked locked.
