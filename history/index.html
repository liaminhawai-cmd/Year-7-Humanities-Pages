Ancient Egypt archived

Herodotus on the Nile, a visitor writing much of it at second hand. Built as History 2 before the unit list was checked; Year 7 does not teach an Egypt unit. Kept because it is a complete second-hand-report source and works as extra practice.

The three units are not assessed on the same skill. CAT 1 — Ancient Australia is a source-analysis test: primary and secondary sources, reliability and accuracy, and First Nations beliefs, values and lives. CAT 2 — Ancient Rome is a tourist guidebook for a traveller arriving in 300 CE, marked on chronology, cause and effect, metacognition and presentation — no source analysis at all. CAT 3 — Ancient China is two booklets, chronology skills and source analysis, and adds historical perspectives and historical significance.

So the wall on Ancient Australia rehearses its CAT, and the Vesuvius wall does not rehearse Rome's — it is a source for the unit, not a model of its task.

The booklet and the word list are the same for every topic, so they sit on each topic page rather than here.
