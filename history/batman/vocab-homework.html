What the set is for

One job: students know the words. The morphology, the cloze and the upgrade task are three ways of handling the same words, not three skills being taught at once.

The unit runs eight lessons at about three lessons a week, so homework goes out three times, not eight, matching the real teaching cadence rather than one sheet per lesson. Worksheet 1 covers Lessons 1 to 3, Worksheet 2 covers Lessons 4 to 6, Worksheet 3 covers Lessons 7 and 8 plus a review of all forty-five words.

The words come from the key words gathered across the eight lessons, checked against the Year 7 History Tier 2 master list so nothing is taught twice under two headings. Where the two disagree, the master list wins on placement: civilisation and fertile are on the Lesson 2 list but the master list teaches them in Ancient China, so this set flags them and moves on.

Why nobody can spend three hours on it

Two things do that work, and neither is a rule about effort.

The worksheet is fixed. Five parts in the same order every time, split into a short block per lesson where the part needs it, minutes printed on each block. A student who has done Worksheet 1 knows what Worksheet 3 will ask, so the time goes into the words rather than into working out the task.

The only open-ended part has a word cap. The Upgrade task is one rewrite of no more than 50 words, once per lesson. A capped rewrite cannot become an essay, and a student who writes two or three sentences has finished it properly rather than skipped it. Thirty-five was drafted first and rejected: it is a stronger time constraint, but a cap that is hard to hit with two prescribed words in the sentence stops being a constraint and starts being a trap.

Every block also carries a stop instruction: if it runs past its minutes, draw a line under where you got to, move on, and hand the sheet in with the line on it. The line is information, not a failure. It is the only way to find out which part is mis-timed.

How the Upgrade task uses the wall

The Upgrade task is the wall's move, not the wall's source. A student is given a weak sentence in the Grade 5 register the wall already models, and upgrades it. Two things keep it on rails.

  • The words are given. Two starred words from that lesson. Not "use some good vocabulary".
  • One rubric row is named, and only one. The row rotates so each of the CAT's four criteria is rehearsed twice across the eight lessons, and no lesson asks for all four at once except the last. Lesson 1 names no row, because in Lesson 1 the task is the words.

All eight lessons upgrade a sentence about that lesson's own content, not about the wall's sources. An earlier draft put Lessons 2 and 7 on the Burtt painting so that the wall's source stayed in view across the term; that was reversed. Funerary, ritual and Songlines have nowhere natural to sit in a sentence about 1835.

The move still transfers to the wall, because it is the same move and the rubric row is named on the sheet. What the homework no longer does is rehearse it on the source students will actually see. That is the cost of the decision, and it is the thing to watch for when the first worksheets come back.

Worked example, Lesson 4. The student is given this:

Aboriginal people got food from plants. They looked after the land.

with Historical interpretations named, and reciprocal sustainable prescribed. One possible upgrade, at 34 words, comfortably inside the cap:

Evidence of replanted tubers and managed grasslands suggests a reciprocal relationship with Country: what was taken was returned. That is what made the food supply sustainable across thousands of years.

Worksheet 3 ends with a review grid, not a ninth lesson: all forty-five words in one table, one instruction. Tick every word you could use correctly in a sentence today, circle the five you are least sure of, and revise those five. That grid is also what the vocab hub's printable report should produce, so a student who works online and a student who works on paper hand in the same thing.

What is left out

Fourteen words from the master list's Unit 1 have no home in these worksheets: adapt, theory, era, chronological, resource, technique, innovation, environment, authentic, cause, consequence, impact, document, reveal. Three worksheets across eight lessons has no room for them. Either the unit runs a consolidation lesson, or they stay with the wall and the Word Builder rather than the homework.

The rest of the master list's Unit 1 is already taught elsewhere and was left alone on purpose: source, evidence, interpretation, artefact, archaeologist and excavate are in the shared source-analysis table that every topic on this site reads, and context, perspective, contested and purpose are in the Batman wall's own glossary.

What would be useful to hear back

1. Are these the right forty-five words?

This is the corroboration step, and it is the one thing that could change every worksheet. Lesson 5 in particular is thin.

2. Is fifty minutes right for one sitting?

Each worksheet now covers two or three lessons at once. Nothing has been timed with a class yet, and Part B is the one most likely to run over, because a student who has never split a word before will not do it in a minute or two.

3. Are the fourteen homeless Tier 2 words a problem?

They can have a consolidation lesson, or they can stay with the wall. Both are defensible and it is a teacher's call.

4. What has to happen before Lessons 3, 6 and 8 go anywhere near a class?

They name the Dreaming, funerary practice and sacred sites. The Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung consultation item on the wall is still open and this material sits closer to it than the wall does.
