Vocabulary homework, Weeks 1 to 8

Eight sheets, six words a week, matched to lessons L1 to L8. Thirty minutes each, with the same five parts every week.

Draft. Not taught, not proofread, no answer key.

This page is here to be argued with. It is not in the site navigation and no student can reach it. Three things in it need a teacher before anything is printed: the word sets have not been corroborated against Liam's list, the dates in Week 1 and the ochre claim in Week 6 are not confirmed, and Weeks 3, 6 and 8 need the same cultural check that is still open on the Batman wall.

What the set is for

One job: students know the words. The morphology, the cloze and the upgrade task are three ways of handling the same six words, not three skills being taught at once.

The words come from the key words gathered across the eight Ancient Australia lessons, checked against the Year 7 History Tier 2 master list so nothing is taught twice under two headings. Where the two disagree, the master list wins on placement: civilisation and fertile are on the L2 lesson list but the master list teaches them in Ancient China, so this set flags them and moves on.

Why nobody can spend three hours on it

Two things do that work, and neither is a rule about effort.

The sheet is fixed. Five parts, same five every week, same order, minutes printed on each one. A student who has done Week 1 knows what Week 5 will ask, so the time goes into the words rather than into working out the task.

The only open-ended part has a word cap. Part D is one rewrite of no more than 35 words. A capped rewrite cannot become an essay, and a student who writes two sentences has finished it properly rather than skipped it.

Every sheet also carries a stop instruction: if a part runs past its minutes, draw a line under where you got to, move on, and hand the sheet in with the line on it. The line is information, not a failure. It is the only way to find out which part is mis-timed.

How Part D uses the wall

Part D is the wall's move, not the wall's source. A student is given a weak sentence in the Grade 5 register the wall already models, and upgrades it. Two things keep it on rails.

  • The words are given. Two starred words from that week's six. Not "use some good vocabulary".
  • One rubric row is named, and only one. The row rotates so each of the CAT's four criteria is rehearsed twice across the eight weeks, and no week asks for all four at once. Week 1 names no row, because in Week 1 the task is the words.

Weeks 2 and 7 upgrade a sentence about the Burtt painting, which is the source on the wall in front of them. The other six upgrade a sentence about the lesson's own content, because funerary and ritual live in a sentence about Songlines, not in a sentence about 1835.

Worked example, Week 4. The student is given this:

Aboriginal people got food from plants. They looked after the land.

with Historical interpretations named, and reciprocal sustainable prescribed. One possible upgrade, at 34 words:

Evidence of replanted tubers and managed grasslands suggests a reciprocal relationship with Country: what was taken was returned. That is what made the food supply sustainable across thousands of years.

One extra sheet at the end, not a ninth week: all forty-eight words in one grid, with a single instruction. Tick every word you could use correctly in a sentence today, circle the five you are least sure of, and revise those five. That grid is also what the vocab hub's printable report should produce, so a student who works online and a student who works on paper hand in the same thing.

What is left out

Fourteen words from the master list's Unit 1 have no home in these eight sheets: adapt, theory, era, chronological, resource, technique, innovation, environment, authentic, cause, consequence, impact, document, reveal. Six a week across eight weeks has no room for them. Either the unit runs a consolidation week or two, or they stay with the wall and the Word Builder rather than the homework.

The rest of the master list's Unit 1 is already taught elsewhere and was left alone on purpose: source, evidence, interpretation, artefact, archaeologist and excavate are in the shared source-analysis table that every topic on this site reads, and context, perspective, contested and purpose are in the Batman wall's own glossary.

What would be useful to hear back

1. Are these the right forty-eight words?

This is the corroboration step, and it is the one thing that could change every sheet. Week 5 in particular is thin.

2. Is thirty minutes right, and is 35 words too tight?

Part D at 35 words is deliberately hard to overrun. It may be hard to hit as well. Fifty would be a safer cap and a weaker constraint.

3. Should Part D always use the wall's sources, or the lesson's content?

It currently does both: the wall's painting in Weeks 2 and 7, the lesson's own content in the other six. The alternative is all eight on the painting, which rehearses the CAT harder but strands the topic words.

4. Are the fourteen homeless Tier 2 words a problem?

They can have a consolidation week, or they can stay with the wall. Both are defensible and it is a teacher's call.

5. What has to happen before Weeks 3, 6 and 8 go anywhere near a class?

They name the Dreaming, funerary practice and sacred sites. The Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung consultation item on the wall is still open and this material sits closer to it than the wall does.
